The Pit

About 2,000 simulated traders share one NQ-style futures order book. No price is fed in: it comes out of their orders. Change who is in the room and the market's behaviour changes with it.

    Does it behave like a real market?

    A market model earns trust only if it reproduces the statistical habits of real prices, the "stylized facts" (Cont, 2001). These are measured on the simulated one-minute bars and compared with typical values for index futures.

    Volatility clusters: autocorrelation by lag

    Who survives

    Over many sessions, momentum and value funds that lose 40% are shut down and replaced, and 1% of funds switch strategy each day, with new money leaning toward whichever strategy earned more over the last month. Prop accounts that blow are replaced by new traders every morning.

    How lives ended

    By session since the account started: share already blown (red), share that has taken at least one payout (gold).

    Your agent is a price taker: its orders are filled at the bar's price plus a quarter-point of slippage and don't move the simulated market. When a bar touches both the stop and the target, the stop is assumed to come first. Fees are $0.50 per micro per side.

    How it works

    Every 5 simulated seconds, liquidity is refreshed, market makers requote, and each trader that is due to act sends orders into a limit order book. Market orders walk the book, so price impact, spreads and stop cascades come out of the queue itself.

    Built in (assumptions)

    Fundamental value is a random walk (about 1% a day) with news jumps that arrive in bursts. About 85% of a news move is priced in straight away by quote revisions; the rest has to be traded in by value funds.

    Background liquidity refills toward a depth profile around the last trade and thins out when volatility rises. Order flow is heavier at the open and close. Market orders cannot walk more than 12 points (like exchange protection points).

    The options book is stylised: open interest is bunched around the opening price, heavier on round strikes, with one implied volatility (20%) and expiry about a day and a third away. Dealers re-hedge their delta every 15 seconds.

    Trait ranges (lookbacks, thresholds, stops, sizes) are hand-chosen, not measured. Contract values follow NQ ($20 a point).

    Emergent (not programmed)

    The price path, spreads, depth, stop runs, flash moves, the volatility clustering and fat tails in the table above, which accounts blow up, and which fund strategy grows.

    Known limits

    One instrument, no overnight session (the open gaps by part of the overnight value change). Dealers' option P&L is not tracked. Market makers' quotes are refreshed only every 5 seconds, so they get picked off more than real ones would. Each run is one random history; compare regimes across several seeds, not from a single run. This is a lab for comparing rules, not a forecast of real prices.
