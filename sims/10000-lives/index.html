How Sam's world works

Every life runs one year at a time from 18 until death. All money is in today's dollars, roughly Canadian. Each life gets its own luck: inborn talent, temperament and health genes, plus its own run of market years, recessions, house prices, layoffs, illness, divorce and inheritances.

  • Income depends on education, talent, the city (big city pays 30% more), years of experience and career style. Job-hoppers climb faster but get laid off more. Recessions triple layoff risk.
  • Spending covers rent or a mortgage, kids, debt payments and living costs. Sam saves the chosen share of after-tax income only when the bills are covered. Debt that grows out of control ends in bankruptcy.
  • Investments: a savings account earns 0.5% a year after inflation. Index funds average about 5% with occasional crashes. Stock picking and crypto swing wildly: rare jackpots, deeper crashes, and a typical result below the index.
  • Homes can be bought once Sam has 12% of the price saved and the payments fit the budget. Big-city prices are higher and rise faster.
  • Retirement is paid for from savings, government pensions from 65 (with a minimum income top-up, like GIS) and, for long-time employees, a workplace pension.
  • Life satisfaction each year, on a 0 to 10 scale, adds up Sam's natural temperament, spending (with diminishing returns), marriage, divorce, young and grown children, job fit, layoffs, illness, money stress, commuting and retirement.
  • Lifespan follows real-world death rates by age, adjusted for exercise, health genes, illness, marriage and poverty.
  • Drugs: each Sam has one drug they would use if the chance came. A habit starts from a specific origin (a friend's offer, curiosity, a cousin, coworkers, a partner, a crisis, or for heroin sometimes a painkiller prescription), most often in the late teens and twenties. Regular users can become dependent at real-world rates, recover, relapse and overdose. Heavy drinking and smoking raise the risk of fatal disease over decades, and quitting before 40 removes most of the smoking risk. Opioid addiction is the deadliest, and methadone treatment cuts that risk sharply. Addiction costs money, jobs and marriages.
  • Crime can start from a friend, a recruiter, a layoff, debt or a drug habit. Petty crime (theft, small-time dealing, scams) and serious crime (robbery, trafficking, large fraud) bring in money but carry arrest, legal fees, prison, rivals and victims who fight back. Prison stops income and strains marriages, and the risk of overdose is highest just after release. Most criminals age out by their mid-30s. Every Sam can also be the victim of other people's crimes.
  • Policing is a setting for the whole world. More policing means fewer victims and murders and more arrests, including more charges for minor offences and drug possession. The direction follows research on police force size; the exact sizes are estimates.

The numbers are rough, chosen to look like the real world, but this is a model and not a forecast. It shows how these forces combine. It can't tell anyone what their own life will look like.
