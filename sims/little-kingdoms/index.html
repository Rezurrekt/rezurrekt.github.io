    How the world works

    Land and food. Each town farms the land around it. Grassland and river valleys feed the most people, forest and hills less, and desert almost none unless a river runs through it. Harvests rise and fall with the climate, and droughts or a cold century can bring famine. Better tools in later eras feed more people from the same fields.

    Towns and trade. Crowded towns send settlers to found new ones. Caravans carry food from full granaries to hungry towns, and roads form where they travel most. Trade also carries knowledge, faith and plague. From the Medieval era, ships carry settlers and goods overseas.

    Rulers. Every realm has a ruler with traits such as ambitious, cruel, wise or pious, and those traits change what the realm does. Rulers age, have children and die. A child may inherit, a cousin may step in, or the nobles may crown a new dynasty, and sometimes a dead ruler with no heir means civil war. History gives each ruler an epithet after they die.

    War. Neighbours whose borders rub, whose faiths differ or whose rulers are ambitious grow hostile. A realm attacks only when it thinks it's stronger. Armies march along roads, fight battles where they meet, and besiege towns; walls make sieges longer. Wars end in exhaustion or when a side collapses, and conquered towns stay with the winner.

    Rebellion. Towns grow restless when they're far from the capital, recently conquered, hungry, or ruled by a different people or faith. Restless towns may break away and form their own realm, and towns cut off for long enough become a new people with their own names.

    Faith, eras, disasters. Prophets found new faiths that spread town to town, and realms convert when most of their people do. Knowledge grows with population and trade, moving realms from the Tribal age to Gunpowder. Plagues, droughts, cold centuries and nomad hordes shake things up. Nothing is scripted: every world plays out differently.
